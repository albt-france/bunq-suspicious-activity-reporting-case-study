What the data shows

  1. bunq reports 280 FTE in onboarding, support and compliance (42% of staff) and fee income of €3.26m from France/Belgium customers (as stated by bunq).
  2. Tracfin: 93% of French reports come from the financial sector, banks +45%; its stated challenge is quality.
  3. Four transparent rules reach 3.7× the base rate on labelled synthetic data, but miss 87% of laundering accounts.
  4. In the simulated desk, a new rule doubled time to file in spring; a schema change cut completeness at draft to 83%; a clock change faked a -40% gain.
  5. The pre-fill cut analyst effort by 23% (DiD; truth 25%).

Recommendations for a France reporting desk

  1. Quality before volume (Tracfin's own message for 2025): block drafts with missing required fields; track completeness at draft, not only at submission.
  2. One definition per KPI: time to file starts at the alert; deadlines on Paris dates; reports counted once per report_id.
  3. Never credit a step change without a cause: the September "-40%" was a clock change; the real gain was the August pre-fill (-23% effort).
  4. Measure what the law cares about: pre-execution reports, follow-ups and the 5-working-day FIU request clock of AMLR art. 69.
  5. Close CAP items with evidence; review overdue items weekly by owner team.

Why it matters for a France desk

More reports do not mean better reporting. A desk that files fast but incomplete reports adds work for the FIU. The KPIs on the next pages therefore weigh completeness and correctness as much as speed.

France also has more payment fraud than the Netherlands, bunq's home market: card fraud of 4.7 vs 2.1 basis points of value in 2025-S2; French credit-transfer fraud reached €249m in 2025-S2, +28% in a year.

What four simple rules do

  • Together they flag 18,446 accounts (36 per 1,000).
  • Precision 4.6%: 3.7× the base rate of 1.2%, but 95% of alerts are false.
  • Recall 13%: most laundering accounts are never flagged.
  • At 20 minutes per alert (assumption), 7.3 analyst hours per true positive.

Account-level evaluation; thresholds fixed before looking at the labels. Rules: fan-out and fan-in (8+ counterparties a week), structuring (3+ payments of USD 8.5-10k a week), rapid pass-through (80-100% sent on the same day).

So what for reporting

Rules are a funnel input, not the answer: the reporting desk inherits their noise. That is why triage capacity, alert-to-case rates and time to file must be read together, and why a new rule (page 4) shows up first as a backlog.

Reports to the FIU should carry the pattern (typology, links between transactions) because single-transaction views miss cycles and multi-hop schemes.

Synthetic data: no real customers, no names. Only aggregates are published.

Reading the three stories

  • New rule (March): real operational effect. Alerts 1,519 → 2,639 a month; median time to file 12 → 24 days; on time 78% → 5%.
  • December: a seasonal inflow of scam alerts, not a process failure. Plan capacity, do not open a CAP item.
  • September: a definition artefact. The -40% is not credited. Always recompute from raw timestamps and check the clock start.

AMLR: what changes on 10 July 2027

  • Art. 69: report suspicions to the FIU and reply to its requests within 5 working days (may be shortened or extended).
  • Art. 69(3): AMLA standards on the report format (draft due 10 July 2026); art. 69(5): guidelines on indicators by 10 July 2027.
  • Art. 71: refrain from executing suspected transactions until reported.
  • Art. 73: prohibition of disclosure. Art. 77: keep the assessment, reported or not.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, EUR-Lex text, articles checked on 18 Nov 2026.

Methods

  • SQL (DuckDB; CTEs and window functions): IBM profile and rules; funnel, timeliness, backlog, quality, CAP, DiD cells.
  • Rules evaluated at account level: precision, recall, alerts per 1,000 accounts; threshold curve.
  • Simulation: 12 months, 2 desks, seed 42, 8 hidden events found without the answer key.
  • Difference-in-differences on log effort, France vs Benelux, 95% interval from a bootstrap over weeks.
  • Automation: pre-filled draft + blocking completeness check, with tests (missing field, duplicate, midnight, clock).

Assumptions (SIMULATED)

  • France desk 55 alerts a day, Benelux 40; 75% rules, 20% internal referrals, 5% external requests.
  • 28-90% of alerts become cases by source; 40% of cases become reports.
  • Internal target 14 calendar days from alert to submission: a simulated rule, not a legal deadline.
  • 20 minutes to review an alert (cost per true positive).
  • Volumes are plausible for an online bank; they are not bunq's.

Limits

  • No real bunq data: pages 4-6 show how the methods behave, not bunq's performance, processes or failures.
  • IBM data is synthetic; real laundering is rarer, more varied and adaptive.
  • Legal points only from the sources read; French code articles cited as stated by Tracfin. Not legal advice: to be confirmed with Compliance / Legal.
  • Looker files are illustrative and not validated in a Looker instance.
